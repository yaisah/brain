# Test the promise before committing

Use this optional exercise when a proposal creates a new offering or workflow, consumes a material investment, makes a difficult-to-reverse commitment, or depends on unusually uncertain customer value. A routine improvement with understood value usually does not need it.

Offer the exercise when it would clarify the decision; use it when the user accepts or explicitly requests it. Follow the invoking skill's project selection and privacy rules before reading any project evidence. This reference does not select a project or grant additional permissions.

## Start from a future user outcome

Read the available discovery, alternatives, constraints, and business assumptions. Write a short account of a customer successfully using the proposed offering: their starting situation, the progress they make, and the friction that disappears.

Treat the account as a scenario to test. Do not pretend the offering has launched, that customers supplied quotations, or that projected benefits have occurred. Avoid names, dates, prices, and guarantees unsupported by the selected project's evidence.

## Interrogate the promise

Ask what a skeptical customer would need to know before switching: the benefit, effort, cost, trust, limitations, and alternatives. Then ask what the organization must know before making that promise: ability to deliver, operating burden, route to customers, economic viability, and how success or failure would be measured.

Separate unanswered questions that merely improve the proposition from assumptions whose failure would invalidate it. Connect every critical assumption to evidence or a specific test. A persuasive scenario is not evidence that demand exists.

## Reach a bounded judgment

Choose one advisory outcome: proceed to the next planning step, revise the proposition, or gather evidence first. Explain the assumptions controlling that outcome and the condition that would change it.

Use `templates/workflows/working-backwards.md`, resolved from the toolkit root, and save `outputs/YYYY-MM-DD_working-backwards_<topic>.md` inside the selected project. Link the exercise from the business case or product brief rather than copying its full contents.

If the outcome calls for revision or more evidence, keep the parent artifact's unresolved risks and readiness state explicit. This exercise does not authorize a build, launch, external announcement, or strategy change.
